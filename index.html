Can Dogs Eat Apricots? Fresh Flesh Only — Never the Pit

By HappyPupGuide

Yes — a small piece of fresh apricot flesh is safe for most dogs. Apricots are sweet, soft and easy to chew, and a few small pieces make a nice occasional treat. The rule that matters is simple: flesh only, pit always removed. The pit, stem and leaves of an apricot are not safe for dogs, so they must never reach the bowl.

The pit is the danger in two ways. It is hard and slippery enough to be a choking risk, especially for small dogs, and it can block the gut if swallowed whole. Apricot pits also contain amygdalin, a compound that can release cyanide when chewed or digested. A dog that crunches a pit should be watched closely, and a vet should be called if the dog seems unwell. Never offer an apricot whole — always cut the fruit open, take the pit out, and throw it away where the dog cannot reach it.

Serve only fresh, ripe apricots. Wash the fruit first, remove the pit, and cut the flesh into small pieces that suit the size of the dog. One or two small pieces are enough for a medium dog, and less for a small dog. Dried apricots are a different matter: they are often treated with sulphur, coated in sugar, or sold with added sweeteners, and they are much more concentrated in natural sugars. If you do offer dried apricot at all, it should be a plain, unsweetened piece in a very small amount — fresh is the safer choice.

Apricots are a treat, not a meal. Treats of any kind should stay a small part of the day's food, with the dog's regular balanced diet doing the main work. Dogs with sensitive stomachs should only try new foods after a vet's okay, and any new food can cause soft stools or gas the first time. If you notice vomiting or diarrhoea, stop and go back to plain food for a day, and speak to your vet if it does not settle.
